\begin{abstract}
Video models spend most of their compute on patches that have not changed, and the rules used to skip them compare a patch with the previous frame and promise nothing about what was discarded. Certified skip replaced that heuristic on fixed cameras by a rule with a theorem: while a patch is skipped, no object of contrast $\Delta$ can have appeared, vanished, split or merged inside it. This paper takes three further steps. (1)~The certificate is extended to a group of warps, a per-frame homography plus per-patch refinement with the reference held in a world canvas. It is exact relative to the constructed reference; the one approximation, bilinear resampling, enters through an observable per-pixel term $\iota$ that is zero on a fixed camera. On the 90 clips of DAVIS~2017 the rule drops a median of 72.8\% of patches against 43.5\% for the best fixed-camera rule, almost all on the background, with no violation in 20{,}242 audited patches. (2)~For any seminorm statistic, the causal anchored rule at threshold $\Delta$ keeps at most twice as many patches as a clairvoyant schedule at $\Delta/2$, plus one, and no deterministic causal rule is competitive with a clairvoyant schedule at any threshold above $\Delta/2$. On footage the rule keeps at most 18\% more than the clairvoyant optimum at the same threshold. (3)~We audit the temporal decision rules of seven published token reducers on planted events at matched budgets. Rules comparing consecutive frames miss slow fades unless the frame rate is low relative to the change; rules with periodic, segment or frame-level anchors see fades but miss brief and small events; the certified rule catches both. Refusing the brightness shift a cached token cannot apply costs under one point of drop rate. Token reuse inside a model and long-video benchmarks are left open.
\end{abstract}
